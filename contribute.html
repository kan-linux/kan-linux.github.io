The implementation of Kan-Linux OS is not fully open-sourced at the moment:

  • The repository hosts Open‑KanLinux, which is the open‑source subset of Kan‑Linux OS. This separation is conceptually similar to the relationship between OpenHarmony (open‑source foundation) and HarmonyOS (commercial product built upon OpenHarmony): the open‑source part exposes base components, while the complete commercial product includes additional closed‑source proprietary layers.
  • Similarly, it is clear that Xiaomi HyperOS will not be fully open-sourced, although its mobile variant is built upon Android.
  • Project Kan-Linux is not a hobby project.
  • We will make Kan-Linux as secure as possible. That said, community contributions to other fully open components (such as Kan-Agent, desktop-shell) or upstream components (such as Hyprland, Quickshell, Strata) of Kan-Linux are still welcome, pls refer to Quick Start.

Contribute to the custom desktop shell

We maintain a customized Omarchy desktop shell that is actively used within Kan-Linux. Your contributions will land in the next Kan-Linux release, or you can manually update the local desktop shell in an installed Kan-Linux in real time.

Your valuable feedback

We welcome feedback via issues for any problems you encounter when testing the LiveISO in virtual machines or real hardware, or after installing Kan-Linux on physical x86-64 laptops and PCs.

Language policy and AI policy

  • To facilitate clear and effective communication, all discussions, comments, documentation and PRs in this project should be conducted in English. English and Chinese are both fine for issues, discussions and wiki.
  • Discussions, issues and PRs should include human-written content. AI-assisted polish is accepted and welcomed (many of us are non-native English speakers). What matters is not who typed the content but whether a human understands it.
  • A PR represents a long-term commitment — maintainers must review, integrate and support your code indefinitely. What matters is not who wrote the code but whether a human understands it: either fully, or at the 70–90% level, and is able to explain major changes to reviewers, even with AI assistance.
  • Fully automated AI-generated content and fully automated PRs are not permitted. We value human connection and communication over fully AI-driven interaction. In other words, Human in the Loop.
